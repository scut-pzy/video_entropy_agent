Reinforcement learning has taught multimodal models to think with videos, zooming into frames or replaying segments, and their tool-call rates and accuracy rise accordingly. Recent research reveals that a minority of high-entropy tokens mark where a model actually deliberates. This offers a direct view of tool use: a call that fetches missing evidence should sit inside the deliberation, and uncertainty should resolve only after the evidence arrives. We find the opposite. On videos whose sparse frames lack the decisive evidence, an RL-trained agent places 62% of its top-entropy tokens before the first call and reasons with less than half that entropy once the evidence returns. Counterfactual returns confirm this: replacing the returned frames with random frames, blank frames, or even the missing evidence leaves 91% of its answers unchanged, whereas removing its premature conclusion restores their dependence on the evidence. Inspired by this, we propose ERA (Entropy-Resolved evidence Acquisition). ERA selects cold-start trajectories whose high-entropy tokens surround the tool call and resolve only for genuine evidence, and then reinforces evidence acquisition under a visual budget. On a VideoNet subset that sparse frames cannot answer but a short localized segment can, ERA improves accuracy by [X] points, and its RL stage recovers [Y] points on the diagnosed agent. By aligning where a model deliberates with where evidence enters, ERA offers a pathway toward video agents that look before they conclude.
