# Abstract（终稿版，299 tokens，2026-09-11）

Reinforcement learning has taught multimodal models to think with videos by zooming into frames or replaying segments, raising both tool-call rates and accuracy. Recent research reveals that a minority of high-entropy tokens mark where a model deliberates, offering a direct view of tool use: a model that calls a tool for missing evidence should stay uncertain until that evidence is observed. The question is therefore not whether uncertainty falls after a tool call, but whether it falls because of what the tool returns. On videos whose sparse frames lack the decisive evidence, RL-trained video agents place 62% of their top-entropy tokens before the first call and often commit to an answer before the evidence arrives. Their entropy then drops sharply, yet this resolution is largely evidence-independent: replacing the returned frames with random frames, blank frames, or even the missing evidence itself leaves 91% of answers unchanged, whereas removing the premature conclusion restores sensitivity to the evidence. Inspired by this, we propose ERA (Entropy-Resolved evidence Acquisition), which selects cold-start trajectories whose uncertainty resolves under genuine but not counterfactual returns, and then reinforces evidence acquisition under a visual budget. On a VideoNet subset that sparse frames cannot answer but a short localized segment can, ERA improves accuracy by [X] points, and its RL stage also improves the diagnosed agents by [Y] points. By asking whether uncertainty is resolved by evidence rather than merely after it, ERA offers a pathway toward video agents that look before they conclude.
